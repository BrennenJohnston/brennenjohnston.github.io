Almost nothing on this site was made alone. Below are the agencies, universities, maker organizations, and individual people I work with, and what each collaboration has produced. For the devices themselves, see [all projects]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}).

## WATAP

I lead the 3D Printed Assistive Technology Program at the [Washington Assistive Technology Act Program (WATAP)](https://watap.org/), which provides [free 3D-printed assistive devices to Washington state residents](https://watap.org/3d-printed-assistive-technology). WATAP is where the design requests come from: someone describes a barrier, and the answer is either an existing model, an adaptation of one, or something new.

Several designs on this site are in WATAP's free catalog, including the [Plug Puller]({{ '/projects/plug-puller-watap/' | relative_url }}), the [Child Car Seat Unbuckle Aid]({{ '/projects/car-seat-unbuckle-aid/' | relative_url }}), and the [WATAP Fidget Rings]({{ '/projects/fidget-rings/' | relative_url }}).

## Sound Transit and Portland State University

WATAP is partnering with Sound Transit and a research team at Portland State University on 3D-printed tactile transit maps that help riders who are blind or have low vision navigate train stations by touch. The university and transit teams have been researching tactile map symbology for Seattle-area stations; my part is addressing the reading difficulties faced by people with coexisting disabilities such as neuropathy, which can make swell-paper and thermoformed maps hard to feel.

That work produced the hybrid construction method and the detachable Velcro symbol key documented on the [tactile maps project page]({{ '/projects/tactile-maps/' | relative_url }}), along with a draft quick reference guide I hope to share with other Assistive Technology Act programs nationally.

## UW CREATE and KEMi

Through [UW CREATE](https://create.uw.edu/), the University of Washington's Center for Research and Education on Accessible Technology and Experiences, I contribute to *Integrating Engineering and Rehabilitation Medicine to Improve Healthcare and Assistive Technology Access and Customizability*, a project funded by the [Kren Engineering-based Medicine Initiative (KEMi)](https://kemi.uw.edu/projects) and led by Jennifer Mankoff with Heather Feldner.

My focus in it is accessible documentation standards for DIY and 3D-printed assistive technology, so open designs are easier to share, reproduce, and adapt by the people who need them. Related efforts and repositories are gathered at [a11yhood.org](https://a11yhood.org).

## HuskyADAPT

[HuskyADAPT](https://www.huskyadapt.me.uw.edu/our-mission) is the University of Washington student organization for accessible design and inclusive play, and it houses Washington's GoBabyGo! program. HuskyADAPT hosted much of the hackathon work behind the [OpenSCAD Assistive Forge]({{ '/projects/openscad-assistive-forge/' | relative_url }}) and its [Charm Customizer]({{ '/projects/picture-charm-customizer/' | relative_url }}).

## WOOF3D

[WOOF3D](https://www.woof3d.org/projects) is the University of Washington's student 3D printing club and has been a remarkable partner. Over the last year they have worked through every custom assistive technology request I've sent them, and then some, which is what makes it possible for a request not to sit waiting on one person's schedule.

The [Charm Customizer]({{ '/projects/picture-charm-customizer/' | relative_url }}) is directly descended from a WOOF3D student design, and the [OpenSCAD Assistive Forge]({{ '/projects/openscad-assistive-forge/' | relative_url }}) grew out of it.

## Makers Making Change and TOM

Local capacity only goes so far, so I also work to connect Washington's maker groups to the wider networks: [Makers Making Change](https://www.makersmakingchange.com/s/assistive-devices) across North America and [TOM (Tikkun Olam Makers)](https://tomglobal.org/about) globally. Makers Making Change documentation is in progress for the [Custom Braille Card Embosser]({{ '/projects/braille-card-embosser/' | relative_url }}).

## Hackathons

Two hackathons shaped the tools on this site more than anything else:

- The **Adaptive Solutions Mini-Hackathon**, run with UW CREATE, HuskyADAPT, and the King County Library System.
- The **GitHub Open Source Assistive Technology Hackathon**, whose [project collection is on GitHub](https://github.com/tanvibachu/GitHub-OS-AT-Hackathon-x-HuskyADAPT-UW).

Both are where a single charm request turned into a general-purpose web front end for OpenSCAD assistive design tools.

## Working together

If you are a maker group, clinician, researcher, or someone who uses assistive technology and wants to test or adapt something here, the fastest way in is to comment on the model pages linked from any [project]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}), or to open an issue on [GitHub](https://github.com/BrennenJohnston). Prototypes are shared early on purpose: real feedback from users, clinicians, and makers is what turns a rough prototype into a device that actually works for people.
