Hi, I'm Brennen! I lead the 3D Printed Assistive Technology Program for WATAP, providing [free 3D-printed assistive devices and resources to Washington state residents](https://watap.org/3d-printed-assistive-technology). I work to connect engineering, medicine, and the disability community, primarily through open-source design. You can learn more about WATAP's wider mission [at watap.org](https://watap.org/).

I've been exploring tools and materials from a young age — over 25 years of general maker experience, including about 4 years specifically in 3D printing across FDM, SLS, and SLA. My background includes undergraduate research with the Washington NASA Space Grant Consortium's summer internship program, where our team conducted research in advanced rocketry. Today I work through a growing maker network — local groups like HuskyADAPT and WOOF3D, North American communities like Makers Making Change, and global ones like TOM (Tikkun Olam Makers) — so that no assistive design request has to wait on one person's bench. If you have a device idea, the fastest way to reach all of these groups at once is [WATAP's Design Challenge Intake Form](https://forms.office.com/Pages/DesignPageV2.aspx?origin=NeoPortalPage&subpage=design&id=W9229i_wGkSZoBYqxQYL0hWbd9GaYGhOhlp2Mf6JlQhUOEhOQUZZNU1TSDJIUlg3NEs4U1M1VEVTRC4u).

## Why open source

Every custom device I build solves one person's problem — but published as an open design, it can solve the same problem for people I'll never meet. Open source also means designs improve: makers remix them, AT professionals report what works in the field, and each iteration gets better.
