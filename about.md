Hi, I'm Brennen! I lead the 3D Printed Assistive Technology Program for WATAP, providing [free 3D-printed assistive devices and resources to Washington state residents](https://watap.org/3d-printed-assistive-technology). I work to connect engineering, medicine, and the disability community, primarily through open-source design. You can learn more about WATAP's wider mission at [watap.org](https://watap.org/).

With over 25 years of hands-on maker experience, I've been exploring tools and materials since my childhood. My technical background includes time on a U.S. Army R&D team, undergraduate research with the Washington NASA Space Grant Consortium, and five years specializing in FDM 3D printing. Today, part of my time is spent growing an assistive technology maker network. By connecting local groups like [HuskyADAPT](https://www.huskyadapt.me.uw.edu/our-mission) and [WOOF3D](https://www.woof3d.org/projects) with continental networks like [Makers Making Change](https://www.makersmakingchange.com/s/assistive-devices) and global communities like [TOM (Tikkun Olam Makers)](https://tomglobal.org/about), I'm working towards making sure no assistive design request ever has to wait on a single person's schedule.

## Additional job responsibilities

A secondary part of my job is working with the [University of Washington CREATE](https://create.uw.edu/) group (the Center for Research and Education on Accessible Technology and Experiences). There are a lot of work-in-progress projects I'm participating in, but a [KEMi](https://kemi.uw.edu/projects) project funds my working time separate from the AT program. Our funded project is called *Integrating Engineering & Rehabilitation Medicine to Improve Healthcare & Assistive Technology*, and I'm working on finding a more accessible documentation standard for makers and users of assistive technology.

We've also established a few local partnerships with various maker groups in order to offload a majority of the custom 3D print design requests that we receive. [WOOF3D](https://www.woof3d.org/projects) is the UW 3D printing club, and has been a fantastic partner this last year; they've worked through every custom AT request I've received and then some. [HuskyADAPT](https://www.huskyadapt.me.uw.edu/our-mission) houses our state's GoBabyGo! program, and has many additional programs that create and develop new AT.
