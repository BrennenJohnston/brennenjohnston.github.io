This site makes a few short sounds when you press, scroll or type, so that you can hear what you just did. Every sound is a recording of a real thing, made by a person and shared for others to use. None was made by a computer.

The switch at the top of every page reads "Sounds: on" or "Sounds: off". Pressing it turns every sound off or on, and your choice is kept on this device. Nothing on this site needs sound: each sound has a twin you can see, listed with it below.

## What you hear

The players below play each recording at one loudness. On the site, the sounds you make most often are the quietest.

## What stays silent

- Moving keyboard focus, with Tab or with a screen reader's keys, so that the site never talks over a screen reader.
- Pointing at a link in the text. Only buttons answer the pointer.
- A scroll the page makes by itself, such as the skip link or a jump to a heading.
- The browser's Back and Forward.
- A key held down: one press, one sound.
- Turning sounds off. The switch's new label is the confirmation.
- Anything before your first press or key on a page. Browsers do not let a page make sound until then.

## When sounds start off

Sounds start off, until you turn them on, when your device asks for reduced motion or more contrast, or uses a Windows contrast theme. Once you press the switch, your choice is what counts.

## What I know is not perfect

- A press sound stops when the page changes, so a quick press can cut it short.
- To keep pages light, the sounds are fetched the first time you move the mouse, touch the page or press a key. If the first thing you do on a visit is a press, that one press may be silent.
- The arrival sound plays only in browsers that allow it.

If a sound gets in your way, the switch turns them all off. I would like to hear about it too: the [accessibility statement]({{ '/accessibility/' | relative_url }}) says how to reach me.
